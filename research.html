Published research

The evidence behind the problem.

Research on health information, patient understanding and clinical oversight, with the population and limits of each finding made clear.

Reviewed 24 October 2026 · Newest publications first. Fieldwork dates are shown separately.

  1. Published study01
    48.4%

    Uncertainty about health information in Indian schools

    In a five city school study, 1,129 of 2,331 students were unsure whether health information shared on social media was accurate and reliable.

    India · students in 75 selected schools · Published 3 October 2026

    Cureus · Project Info Suraksha ↗

    Study scope and limitations
    Sample
    2,331 students in grades 9 to 12 across 75 schools in Ahmedabad, Lucknow, Guwahati, Bhubaneswar and Visakhapatnam. Table 5 reports 1,129 unsure responses.
    Fieldwork
    4 May to 30 July 2023
    Source
    Social Media Literacy and Health Information Seeking Behavior Among School Students and Educators in India: A Needs Assessment Under Project Info Suraksha
    • The article was published in October 2026, but the data were collected in 2023.
    • Selected schools in five cities do not constitute a nationally representative sample of Indian adults or patients. The finding measures students’ perceptions.
  2. Published study02
    34%

    AI is already part of health information seeking

    US adults reported having used chatbots for at least one of eight health purposes, such as understanding symptoms, treatments, diagnoses or lab results.

    US adults · national survey · Published 25 August 2026

    Pew Research Center ↗

    Study scope and limitations
    Sample
    3,488 US adults surveyed by Pew Research Center; the measure covers ever using AI for at least one of eight listed health or medical reasons.
    Fieldwork
    22 to 28 June 2026
    Source
    From Diagnoses to Treatments, Why Americans Use AI Chatbots for Health
    • This is an ‘ever used’ measure, not monthly or daily usage. It should not be compared directly with surveys using a different time window.
    • Adoption and perceived helpfulness do not establish medical accuracy, patient harm or benefit. The population is US adults.
  3. Published study03
    44%

    Limited confidence in judging AI health information

    US adults said they were not confident they could distinguish true from false health information supplied by AI tools or chatbots.

    US adults · national survey · Published 17 June 2026

    KFF ↗

    Study scope and limitations
    Sample
    2,480 US adults surveyed online and by telephone in English and Spanish; overall sampling margin of error ±3 percentage points.
    Fieldwork
    7 to 31 May 2026
    Source
    KFF Tracking Poll on Health Information and Trust: Use of Social Media and AI For Health Information and Advice
    • The percentage combines ‘not too’ and ‘not at all’ confident. It measures confidence, not demonstrated accuracy or actual exposure to harmful advice.
    • This US survey does not establish a prevalence rate for India.
  4. Published study04
    97%

    Doctors stepping in after online misinformation

    Surveyed Canadian physicians reported having to step in after patients acted on inaccurate online health advice, either to avert harm or manage its effects. The question included AI advice.

    Canadian physicians · Physician Pulse survey · Published 21 April 2026

    CMA / Abacus Data ↗

    Study scope and limitations
    Sample
    645 practising physicians in Canada, recruited through Canadian Medical Association outreach; results were weighted by region of practice.
    Fieldwork
    6 to 13 April 2026
    Source
    Disconnected health systems and misinformation are putting patient care at risk, say Canadian physicians
    • This is physicians’ reported experience, not an audited count of injuries or an estimate of how many patients were harmed.
    • This reports experience of intervention, not the share of appointments or patients affected. The sample is Canadian.
  5. Published study05
    54%

    Uncertainty when health advice conflicts

    US adults reported at least some difficulty deciding which information to trust when they encountered conflicting health advice.

    US adults · national survey · Published 7 April 2026

    Pew Research Center ↗

    Study scope and limitations
    Sample
    5,111 US adults in Pew Research Center’s American Trends Panel; weighted to represent the US adult population.
    Fieldwork
    20 to 26 October 2025
    Source
    Where Do Americans Get Health Information, and What Do They Trust?
    • This measures reported difficulty, including ‘somewhat difficult’; it is not a test of whether respondents correctly identified misinformation.
    • The finding describes US adults and is not an estimate for India. Publication in 2026 follows survey fieldwork in 2025.
  6. Published study06
    No improvement

    Choosing the next step requires more than model knowledge

    In a randomized UK study, participants using three LLMs did no better than the control group at selecting the appropriate next action in simulated medical scenarios.

    UK adults · randomized scenario study · Published 9 February 2026

    Nature Medicine ↗

    Study scope and limitations
    Sample
    1,298 adults contributed 2,400 interactions across ten scenarios. The study compared GPT 4o, Llama 3 and Command R+ with participants’ usual resources.
    Fieldwork
    21 August to 14 October 2024
    Source
    Reliability of LLMs as medical assistants for the general public: a randomized preregistered study
    • The next step comparison found no statistically significant difference; identifying relevant conditions was a separate outcome.
    • The study used 2024 models and simulated scenarios. It did not assess current models, real patient outcomes or DigiDr.
  7. Published study07
    62.5%

    Medical claims with very low or no supporting evidence

    Researchers assigned their lowest evidence grade to 193 of 309 assessed claims in a selected sample of health professional videos about cancer and diabetes.

    Selected YouTube videos · cancer and diabetes · Published 16 January 2026

    JAMA Network Open ↗

    Study scope and limitations
    Sample
    309 eligible videos, each with at least 10,000 views and a specific health claim; searches used Korean terms, with Korean and English language videos eligible.
    Fieldwork
    Video search and data capture: 20 to 21 June 2025
    Source
    The Quality of Evidence of and Engagement With Video Medical Claims
    • This selected sample does not represent all online medical content, all specialties or all countries.
    • A low evidence grade does not by itself establish that a claim is false or that it caused patient harm.
