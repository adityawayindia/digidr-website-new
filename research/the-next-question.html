The Next Question Is the Product

A patient rarely arrives with a perfectly structured case. The quality of the next question can matter as much as the quality of the eventual answer.

Ask for information that changes useful understanding: not information the system merely knows how to collect.

Strong model performance does not guarantee a strong interaction

Public use of a model introduces missing information, misunderstanding and conversational failure modes that are absent from a fully specified benchmark prompt.

Fixed forms solve a different problem

A fixed form is efficient when required fields are stable. It becomes wasteful when many questions are irrelevant or already answered in the patient's own words.

Model the question as a decision

A candidate question is useful when possible answers would materially improve an allowed next step: preparation, practice fit, chronology or doctor involvement.

Measure burden as well as completeness

A longer interaction can collect more data while making the product worse. Privacy cost, time, abandonment and unnecessary questions belong in the evaluation.
